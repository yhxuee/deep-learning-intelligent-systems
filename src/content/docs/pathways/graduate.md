Graduate study begins with a diagnostic, not an assumption that prior course titles imply mastery. Students attempt the AI-off checkpoint of each relevant Core unit. A failed checkpoint routes to specific sections and labs; it is not a reason to repeat an entire introductory course.

## Shared competence before specialization

Every student should be able to:

- derive likelihood, backpropagation, attention, and the uncertainty calculation used in their work;
- trace memory, arithmetic, and data provenance through an experiment;
- distinguish a behavioural result from a mechanism claim;
- state an estimand and experimental unit before selecting a statistical procedure;
- use AI with an explicit task contract, artifact trail, and independent verifier;
- defend the strongest plausible alternative explanation.

A diagnostic pass requires reconstruction and transfer, not recognition of vocabulary.

## Three specializations

| Specialization | Core emphasis | Research construction | Verification emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Models and learning algorithms** | 02–05, 07–09, 11 | new representation, objective, memory mechanism, or training method | matched baselines, optimization budget, ablations, rival mechanism predictions |
| **Computational learning systems** | 03–06, 08, 10 | efficient operator, low-precision or distributed method, inference system | numerical error, hardware-aware accounting, end-to-end latency, regression and stress tests |
| **Scientific ML and autonomous systems** | 01, 05, 08–12 | inverse problem, neural operator, controller, world model, or AI-assisted discovery workflow | data-generating assumptions, physical or causal constraints, uncertainty, external validation, safety gates |

Students may combine tracks, but must name which competence is being deepened rather than collecting unrelated topics.

## Milestones

1. **Reproduction.** Recover one quantitative result from a written description. Record ambiguities and implementation decisions.
2. **Adversarial audit.** Find one failure mode, data problem, or metric loophole. Demonstrate it with the correct units and controls.
3. **Rival hypotheses.** Formulate at least two explanations and choose an experiment on which their predictions diverge.
4. **Registered extension.** Fix the estimand, analysis, minimum meaningful effect, stopping rule, and compute budget before outcome-bearing runs.
5. **Independent replication.** Change the dimension most likely to carry a shared error: code, data, analyst, model family, or metric.
6. **Oral defence.** Explain what would falsify the claim, why the verifier is adequate, and what remains outside scope.

AI may scale execution at every milestone after the student can state the verification contract. All delegated actions, failures, and human overrides are preserved. Self-review by the same agent does not count as independent replication.

## Capstone acceptance standard

The target is not “a workshop-looking PDF.” A capstone passes when its central claim is inspectable and bounded:

- every main table traces to code, configuration, data, environment, and complete run records;
- implementation has positive, negative, and adversarial controls;
- comparisons use matched tuning and compute or state the mismatch;
- uncertainty matches experimental units and missing runs remain visible;
- exploratory and confirmatory analyses are separated;
- at least one replication attacks a plausible shared failure mode;
- AI use is disclosed at the level needed to reproduce and audit it;
- the oral defence survives an alternative explanation proposed by the examiner.

A novel positive result is not required. A careful null, correction, or boundary finding may be stronger research than a selected improvement. The exit standard is the ability to produce and defend verifiable knowledge, not merely to produce research-shaped text.
