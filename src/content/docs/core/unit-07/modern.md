## 7.8.1 Web-scale filtering pipelines

**Quantity:** which sequences receive gradient.

Open pretraining datasets now document their pipelines in detail: language identification, heuristic quality filters, model-based quality classifiers, and fuzzy deduplication with MinHash at the scale of trillions of tokens. [Penedo et al. (2024)](/references/#penedo-2024) ablate each step by training small models on the filtered data and measuring downstream performance — data decisions treated as experiments with controls **[E]**. Deduplication improved quality at fixed compute; aggressive deduplication across snapshots of the web did not always help, a reminder that §7.5.5's question has an empirical answer per corpus **[E]**.

## 7.8.2 Data mixtures

**Quantity:** the weights of sources in the training distribution.

A corpus is a mixture of sources (web, code, books, mathematics, dialogue). The mixture weights change which skills receive gradient (§7.5.2). DoReMi learns mixture weights with a small proxy model trained to minimize the worst-case excess loss across domains, then trains the large model on the learned mixture ([Xie et al. 2023](/references/#xie-2023)) **[E]**. Mixtures are increasingly scheduled over training — more general data early, more specialized data late **[O]**.

## 7.8.3 Synthetic and curated data

**Quantity:** information per token.

TinyStories showed that a corpus of simple stories, generated by a larger model with a restricted vocabulary, lets very small models produce fluent text ([Eldan & Li 2023](/references/#eldan-2023)); the spine corpus imitates that idea with a template generator. Models trained on curated "textbook-quality" and synthetic exercises achieved strong coding results at small scale ([Gunasekar et al. 2023](/references/#gunasekar-2023)) **[E]**. The risk: training repeatedly on a model's own outputs narrows the distribution and loses its tails — **model collapse** ([Shumailov et al. 2024](/references/#shumailov-2024)) **[E]**. Mixing in fresh real data mitigates it **[E]**.

## 7.8.4 Predicting representations instead of pixels

**Quantity:** what is hidden, and in which space it is predicted.

Joint-embedding predictive architectures hide parts of an image and predict the *representations* of the hidden parts rather than their pixels ([Assran et al. 2023](/references/#assran-2023)) **[E]**. The motivation is the one in §7.2.1: predicting every pixel spends capacity on unpredictable detail; predicting in representation space lets the objective ignore it. Collapse then has to be prevented by the asymmetric means of §7.5.3 **[O]**.

## 7.8.5 Contamination at scale

**Quantity:** overlap between training data and evaluation data.

Large models are trained on crawls that contain many public benchmarks. Studies document benchmark contamination and its effect on reported scores ([Sainz et al. 2023](/references/#sainz-2023)), and propose statistical tests that detect contamination from model likelihoods alone ([Oren et al. 2024](/references/#oren-2024)) **[E]**. The response in evaluation practice — held-out sets created after a model's training cut-off, canary strings, private test sets — is Unit 10's subject **[O]**.
