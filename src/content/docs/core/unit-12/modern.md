## 12.8.1 Checklists and reporting standards

**Quantity:** what a reader needs to reproduce a result.

Major machine-learning conferences adopted reproducibility checklists — code, data, hyperparameters, compute, number of runs, error bars — after a reproducibility programme found that many submitted results could not be reproduced from the paper alone ([Pineau et al. 2021](/references/#pineau-2021)) **[E]**. Reporting the *expected best result as a function of the tuning budget*, rather than only the best result found, makes comparisons between methods with different tuning effort fair ([Dodge et al. 2019](/references/#dodge-2019)) **[D]/[O]**.

## 12.8.2 Statistics for few runs

**Quantity:** the reliability of conclusions from a handful of seeds.

In deep reinforcement learning, where each run is expensive and results are noisy, point estimates from a few runs led to conflicting conclusions; interval estimates, robust aggregate statistics and performance profiles changed several published rankings ([Agarwal et al. 2021](/references/#agarwal-2021)) **[E]**. The same tools apply to any ablation with few seeds. The American Statistical Association's statement on $p$-values summarizes what significance does and does not mean ([Wasserstein & Lazar 2016](/references/#wasserstein-2016)) **[O]**.

## 12.8.3 Re-evaluations

**Quantity:** how much of reported progress survives equal tuning.

Careful re-evaluations repeatedly find that strong baselines, tuned as hard as the new method, close much of the reported gap: in language modelling ([Melis et al. 2018](/references/#melis-2018)), generative adversarial networks ([Lucic et al. 2018](/references/#lucic-2018)) and reinforcement learning ([Henderson et al. 2018](/references/#henderson-2018)) **[E]**. Leakage and evaluation errors produce a further class of spurious results in applied machine-learning research across many fields ([Kapoor & Narayanan 2023](/references/#kapoor-2023)) **[E]**.

## 12.8.4 AI as a research collaborator

**Quantity:** which steps of the research loop can be delegated, and how they are checked.

Search systems pairing a language model with an automatic evaluator found new mathematical constructions that were verified independently ([Romera-Paredes et al. 2024](/references/#romera-paredes-2024)), and evolutionary coding agents have improved algorithms and engineering systems against automated benchmarks ([Novikov et al. 2025](/references/#novikov-2025)) **[E]**. End-to-end "AI scientist" pipelines generate complete papers at low cost, with quality limited by their ability to check their own experiments ([Lu et al. 2024](/references/#lu-2024)) **[O]**. In a blinded comparison judged by over a hundred NLP researchers, ideas generated by a language model were rated more novel than experts' but slightly less feasible ([Si et al. 2024](/references/#si-2024)) **[E]** — proposing is not the bottleneck. The open question is not whether AI can propose and execute experiments — it can — but how its outputs are verified at the speed they are produced **[H]**.
