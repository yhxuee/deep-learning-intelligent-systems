> **Central proposition.** A textbook should no longer try to be a compressed copy of everything a student could know.
> It should build durable mental models, independent judgment, and the ability to create and verify knowledge with AI.
> **You can delegate only what you can verify — and you can verify only what you have internalized.**

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="The Core" icon="open-book">
    Twelve units, each built around one enduring question and **one concrete exemplar** you internalize completely —
    the Transformer block, reverse-mode autodiff, a linear recurrence, a scaling-law fit.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The Spine project" icon="rocket">

## Three levels of knowledge ownership

| Level | What you achieve | Examples | How mastery is shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| <span class="tier tier-internalize">Internalize</span> | Reconstruct and reason without AI | chain rule, likelihood, attention, gradient descent, generalization | derive, transfer, find counterexamples unaided |
| <span class="tier tier-verify">Understand & verify</span> | Explain mechanism and failure modes; check external answers | optimizer variants, diffusion objectives, parallelism strategies | critique a generated solution; design the check |
| <span class="tier tier-access">Access on demand</span> | Find, contextualize and apply correctly | APIs, model configs, benchmark releases | locate authoritative sources; apply safeguards |
