Mia had a small red kite. Every morning she ran to the hill behind her house to fly it. The wind was strong on the hill, and the kite went up and up until it was a tiny dot in the sky. Mia liked to watch the birds fly next to it.
One day the wind stopped. The kite fell down into a tall tree. Mia looked up at the tree and felt sad. She could not climb so high. She sat on the grass and thought about what to do.
Her friend Leo came by with his dog. "Why are you sad?" he asked. Mia showed him the kite in the tree. Leo looked at the tree for a long time. Then he smiled. "I have an idea," he said.
Leo ran home and came back with a long stick. He held the stick up high, but it was not long enough. The dog barked and ran in circles. Mia laughed. "Your dog wants to help too," she said.
They tied the stick to a broom with some string. Now it was very long. Leo held it up, and Mia helped him push. Slowly, the kite came free and floated down into Mia's hands. She hugged Leo and said thank you.
The next day the wind came back. Mia and Leo flew the kite together, and the dog ran after its shadow on the grass. From then on, they always went to the hill together.
Tom was a little bear who lived in a warm cave. He loved honey more than anything. One morning he found that his honey jar was empty. "Oh no," said Tom. "What will I eat for breakfast?"
Tom walked into the forest to look for honey. He asked the rabbit, but the rabbit only had carrots. He asked the squirrel, but the squirrel only had nuts. Tom kept walking until he heard a soft buzzing sound.
High in an old tree there was a busy hive. The bees flew in and out. Tom wanted the honey, but he did not want to hurt the bees. He sat down and waited. After a while a bee flew down to him.
"Why are you sitting here?" asked the bee. "I am hungry, and I love honey," said Tom. "But I do not want to take it without asking." The bee was surprised. No bear had ever asked before.
The bee went back to the hive and told the others. Soon the bees came down with a little cup of honey for Tom. "Because you asked kindly," they said, "you may have some. Please come back and help us when the rain is heavy."
Tom thanked the bees and went home happy. When the big storm came, Tom held a large leaf over the hive so the rain could not get in. The bees were safe and dry, and Tom always had honey after that.
Sara found a shiny stone by the river. It was blue and smooth and cold in her hand. She put it in her pocket and walked home. At dinner she showed it to her mother. "It is very pretty," said her mother. "Where did you find it?"
Sara told her about the river and the tall green reeds. Her mother said that the river had many stones, and each one had traveled a long way. Sara wondered where her stone had come from. Maybe it had rolled down from the mountains.
The next week Sara went back to the river with a little bag. She found a red stone, a white stone, and a stone with a line across the middle. She lined them up on her window so the sun could shine on them.
Every evening the stones glowed in the last light of the day. Sara gave each one a name. The blue one was called River, because it was the first. She liked to think that the stones were friends who had found each other at last.
Ben had a toy boat that he wanted to sail on the pond. His sister Ana said the boat was too small and would sink. Ben did not agree. He put the boat on the water and gave it a gentle push.
The boat floated! It went slowly across the pond while the ducks swam around it. Ana clapped her hands. "You were right," she said. "It is a good boat." Ben was proud, and he let Ana push the boat back.
Then the wind blew, and the boat sailed to the middle of the pond. It was too far to reach. Ben and Ana waited and waited. At last the wind changed, and the boat came back to the shore, right to their feet.
They picked up the boat and ran home to tell their father. He listened and smiled. "The wind took it away, and the wind brought it back," he said. "Sometimes you just have to be patient."
